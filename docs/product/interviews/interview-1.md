# Interview: Lena K., 29
*German UX Designer, Berlin*

**Q1:** Walk me through the last time you had trouble with measurements on Amazon. Step by step?

**A1:** Okay so, two weeks ago I was looking for a standing desk on Amazon US — there's this brand that doesn't sell on Amazon.de and the reviews were amazing. I open the product page and it says the desktop is "55 by 28 inches." I just stare at it. I have zero intuition for what that means in my apartment. So I open my Google converter tab — I always have one ready — and I type 55 inches, get 139.7 cm, then 28 inches, get 71.1 cm. Then I go to my Notion page where I've written down the dimensions of my office nook and compare. But the listing also had the height range in inches, "28 to 46 inches," so that's two more conversions. By the time I've done all four I've already forgotten which number was the width. It's exhausting, honestly. And this is just one product — I usually compare three or four before deciding.

**Q2:** What's the most frustrating part of how you handle unit conversion today?

**A2:** The context switching. It breaks my whole flow. I'm reading reviews, getting excited about a product, and then I hit the dimensions section and everything stops. I have to go to another tab, type numbers in, come back, try to remember what I just converted. It's like — you know when you're reading a book in a foreign language and you have to look up a word every other sentence? You lose the story. That's what happens to me with Amazon US. I lose the shopping experience. And the worst part is it's not a one-time thing. Every single product, every single session. I've been doing this for two years and it still annoys me the same amount.

**Q3:** What have you tried before? Why did it stop working?

**A3:** I tried Everything Metric — that Chrome extension that's supposed to convert all imperial units on any page. At first I thought, "Finally, someone solved this." But it was terrible. It was converting random numbers on the page that weren't even measurements. Like, a product with 4,200 reviews and suddenly it says "1,905 kg" next to the review count. Or an address with "42" in it getting converted. The Amazon product pages looked broken, like corrupted almost. I'm a designer — I notice these things and they drive me insane. I gave it maybe a week, hoping I'd get used to the false positives, but no. I disabled it and went back to my Google tab workflow. I also briefly tried just doing mental math — multiplying inches by 2.54 — but I'm not fast enough, and that's how I ended up with a bookshelf that was 15 cm too short. That mistake cost me 45 euro in return shipping. So no more mental math.

**Q4:** If a new Chrome extension claims to auto-convert measurements on Amazon — what builds trust? What makes you close the tab?

**A4:** First thing I look at is the Chrome Web Store rating and the number of reviews. If it's below 4 stars or has like 12 reviews, I'm not installing it. Then I look at the screenshots — I want to see it working on an actual Amazon product page, not some generic demo. What builds real trust is if it only converts what it should convert. If I install it and my Amazon page still looks clean, no weird numbers popping up in the title or the price or whatever — that's when I think "okay, these developers actually understand the problem." What makes me close the tab immediately: if the extension wants access to "all websites" or "read and change all your data." I had a bad experience with a coupon extension that was basically spyware. Also if the page looks even slightly broken after installing — any visual glitch, any corrupted text — I'm uninstalling within five minutes. I've been burned before, I don't give second chances anymore.

**Q5:** What would you pay for this? What are you comparing it to?

**A5:** Honestly? I'd pay maybe 2 to 3 euro per month if it actually worked perfectly. I know that sounds low but I'm comparing it to other Chrome extensions and most of them are free. The only extension I've ever paid for is a password manager, and that felt essential for security. This feels more like a quality-of-life thing. But if I think about the 45 euro I lost on that bookshelf return — and the time I waste every week converting numbers — then even 3 euro a month would pay for itself pretty fast. I think a one-time payment of maybe 10 to 15 euro would feel better psychologically though. I hate subscriptions for small tools. If it's free with a premium tier, I'd probably try the free version first and upgrade only if the free one nags me or limits something I need.

**Q6:** Who else in your life deals with this? How differently?

**A6:** My boyfriend Lars, definitely. He orders car parts from US sellers on eBay and Amazon and he's constantly converting torque specs and bolt sizes from imperial to metric. But he's an engineer, so he actually memorized some common conversions — he just knows that a half-inch bolt is roughly 12.7 mm. I could never. My colleague Marta orders fabric from US suppliers and she deals with yards versus meters, which is a whole different headache. She literally printed out a conversion chart and taped it next to her monitor. Like, in 2026. And then there's my mom, who just refuses to buy from Amazon US at all. She says "if they can't show me centimeters, I don't need the product." Which is honestly a valid approach, but she misses out on so much.

**Q7:** Show me your phone — any related apps or tools?

**A7:** Let me grab it... okay so here's my calculator app, I use it sometimes when I'm on the couch browsing Amazon on my laptop and I'm too lazy to open another tab. And here — this is the iOS Measure app, I use it to quickly measure spaces in my apartment when I'm comparing against product dimensions. I don't have a dedicated conversion app actually, I probably should. Oh, and here's my Notion — let me show you this page. I have a table called "Amazon Wishlist" with columns for product name, dimensions in inches as listed, dimensions in cm that I converted, and then a column for "fits?" where I put yes or no. It's embarrassing how much effort I put into this. I built this system because I got tired of converting the same product twice when I came back to it a week later.

**Q8:** What feature would you love that nobody's built?

**A8:** I want inline conversion that's context-aware. Like, it should know that "48 x 24 x 30 inches" on a product page is a dimension and convert it right there, but it should leave the "48" in "48 customer reviews" alone. Nobody seems to get this right. I also want it to show both units — the original inches and the converted centimeters — so I can still reference the original if I need to ask a question in a review or contact the seller. And honestly? A little visual indicator, maybe a subtle highlight or a small "cm" badge, so I can see at a glance which numbers were converted and which are original. As a UX designer I think about these things — I want the conversion to feel native to the page, not like some ugly overlay was slapped on top. Oh, and one more thing — weight conversion too. I never know what "pounds" means for shipping estimates.

**Q9:** What would make you recommend this to a friend?

**A9:** If it just works. That's it, really. If I install it, open Amazon US, and the dimensions are suddenly in centimeters without anything else on the page looking weird — I would message my friend group immediately. I'm not exaggerating. We have a WhatsApp group called "US Shopping Finds" with eight people and we're all dealing with this same problem. The bar is honestly not that high: don't break the page, convert the right numbers, and be accurate. If it also handles weight and maybe temperature for those weird American product descriptions, even better. But the core thing that would make me recommend it is reliability. If it works perfectly 95% of the time and fails gracefully the other 5%, I'm telling everyone. If it works 80% of the time and occasionally turns the product price into kilograms, I'm uninstalling and warning people to stay away.

**Q10:** What am I not asking that I should be?

**A10:** You should ask about the emotional side of this. It sounds silly but there's this feeling of being a second-class customer on Amazon US. The whole site is built for Americans and the rest of us just have to deal with it. When I'm on Amazon.de, everything is in centimeters, everything makes sense, I feel confident buying. On Amazon US I feel uncertain, I second-guess myself, I make spreadsheets to compensate. It shouldn't be this hard. You should also ask about mobile — I sometimes browse Amazon on my phone and there's absolutely no solution for converting measurements there. At least on desktop I can have my Google converter tab. On mobile I'm completely lost. And maybe ask about other measurement types beyond length and weight. Fahrenheit shows up in product descriptions for things like heated blankets or coolers, and I have no idea what "keeps drinks cold at 34°F" means without converting. The problem is bigger than just inches to centimeters.
