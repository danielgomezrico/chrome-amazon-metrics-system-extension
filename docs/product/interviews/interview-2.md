# Interview: Mateo R., 34
*Colombian Software Engineer, Austin TX*

**Q1:** Walk me through the last time you had trouble with measurements on Amazon. Step by step?

**A1:** Oh man, last week actually. I was buying a desk for my home office — you know, one of those standing desks. The listing says "48 x 24 x 50 inches." So I'm standing in my office with a tape measure that has both units, and I'm trying to figure out if 48 inches is going to fit between the bookshelf and the wall. I pull out my phone, "Hey Siri, what's 48 inches in centimeters?" — 121.9 centimeters, okay. Then I do the depth. Then the height. Three separate Siri calls for one product. And the whole time I'm thinking, I *know* what 120 centimeters looks like — I grew up with it. But 48 inches? I have to translate it every single time. It's like reading a book through Google Translate. You get the meaning, but you lose the feel. The worst part is when I'm comparing two desks side by side — I have to convert both to actually *feel* which one is bigger, because inches just don't register spatially for me.

**Q2:** What's the most frustrating part of how you handle unit conversion today?

**A2:** The interruption. Every time I ask Siri or open the calculator, I lose my flow. I'm browsing Amazon, I'm in that comparison mode, reading reviews, checking dimensions — and then I hit a measurement and everything stops. I leave the page mentally. It's not that the conversion is hard — I'm an engineer, I can do basic math — but it's *constant*. It's like a micro-context-switch, and if you're a developer you know how expensive those are. By the time I've converted three dimensions, I've forgotten which product I was comparing against. And the really frustrating thing? I can't trust my mental math when it's something that matters. For the shelf that didn't fit, I was off by like 3 centimeters because I rounded wrong on one dimension. Three centimeters cost me $89 I couldn't get back.

**Q3:** What have you tried before? Why did it stop working?

**A3:** I tried autoConvert — the Chrome extension. Installed it, went to Amazon, and my product page took like eight seconds to load. That's already a no. But the real killer was when it converted a shipping time — "2h30m" or something like that — into centimeters. I was like, *qué es esta vaina*. Uninstalled it within an hour. Before that I tried just bookmarking a unit converter site, but that's even worse than Siri because it's more clicks. I also tried shopping on Amazon Mexico sometimes — they list in metric — but the prices are different, shipping is different, Prime doesn't always apply the same way. My wife suggested I just memorize the common conversions, and honestly I've done that for temperature and a few lengths, but it doesn't scale. There are too many product categories with too many different dimension ranges.

**Q4:** If a new Chrome extension claims to auto-convert measurements on Amazon — what builds trust? What makes you close the tab?

**A4:** First thing I do is check if it's open source. I go straight to the GitHub repo. If there's no repo, I'm already skeptical. I look at the issues — are people reporting bugs? Is the developer responding? I check the permissions it requests. If a unit converter wants access to "all your browsing data" or "read and change all your data on all websites," I close the tab immediately. It should only need access to Amazon pages, maybe a few shopping sites. I also look at the Chrome Web Store reviews, but I weigh the GitHub activity more. Stars, recent commits, whether the README is well-written — that tells me if a real developer maintains it or if it's abandoned. Oh, and if it has fewer than, say, 50 users and no source code, forget it. I've seen too many extensions that are basically spyware with a converter UI on top.

**Q5:** What would you pay for this? What are you comparing it to?

**A5:** Honestly? For a Chrome extension? I'd expect it to be free if it's open source and lightweight. I'd maybe pay $2-3 one time if it was really polished and saved me genuine headaches. But subscriptions for a browser extension? No way. I'm comparing it to... well, nothing really costs money in this space. Siri is free. Calculator apps are free. The broken extensions I tried were free. So the bar for paid is very high. What I *would* pay for is reliability — if I knew it would never break Amazon pages, never misidentify a measurement, and just quietly do its job in the background. That has real value. But I'd need to trust it first, and trust is free to build — just be open source, be lightweight, don't be greedy with permissions.

**Q6:** Who else in your life deals with this? How differently?

**A6:** My wife, Laura — she's also Colombian. She handles it worse than me, honestly. She's not in tech, so she doesn't have the mental math shortcuts. When she's cooking with American recipes, she's constantly asking me to convert cups and ounces to grams. She bought a kitchen scale that does both, which helped for cooking, but shopping online? Same problem. My mom in Bogotá sends us links to things on Amazon sometimes, and she has zero frame of reference for inches. She just trusts whatever I tell her. In my Colombian expat WhatsApp group, this comes up maybe once a month — someone bought the wrong size curtains or whatever. It's a shared pain. Americans don't even realize it's a problem because they've never had to live in their own country feeling like a foreigner in the measurement aisle.

**Q7:** Show me your phone — any related apps or tools?

**A7:** Let me pull it up. So here's my home screen — nothing conversion-specific, honestly. I use Siri probably 80% of the time, it's just the fastest thing. I have the default iOS Calculator, which I use when I need to do compound conversions — like converting cubic inches to liters for a cooler I was buying. I downloaded "Unit Converter" from the App Store like a year ago but I think I used it twice and forgot about it — yeah, it's buried in my App Library somewhere. Oh, and I have the Amazon app obviously, which has the same problem as the website — everything in inches and pounds. The one thing I do that's kind of embarrassing is I have a note in my Notes app called "Conversions" where I wrote down things like "1 inch = 2.54 cm" and "1 lb = 0.45 kg" and a few product-specific ones. It's my cheat sheet.

**Q8:** What feature would you love that nobody's built?

**A8:** Inline conversion that's contextually aware. Not just "find a number followed by 'inches' and replace it." I want something that understands that "48 x 24 x 50 in" is three dimensions of a physical product — length, width, height — and shows me "122 x 61 x 127 cm" right there on the page, maybe in a subtle tooltip or just replacing the text. But it should *not* touch prices, dates, ratings, model numbers — anything that has digits but isn't a measurement. That's where autoConvert failed so badly. And honestly? The dream feature would be showing me a visual size comparison. Like, "this desk is about the width of a standard door." Something that gives me spatial intuition, not just a number in different units. But I'd settle for accurate, quiet inline conversion that doesn't break the page.

**Q9:** What would make you recommend this to a friend?

**A9:** If it just works. That's it. If I install it, go to Amazon, and the dimensions are in metric without me doing anything — and the page still loads fast, and nothing else is broken — I would send it to my WhatsApp group that same day. I'm not exaggerating. There are maybe 40 people in that group who all have this exact problem. The bar for recommendation is actually really low because there's *nothing* good out there right now. It's an unserved need. But the bar for *keeping* the recommendation is high — if one person installs it and it breaks their Amazon or slows things down, I look bad for recommending it, and I'll tell everyone to uninstall. So: fast, accurate, unobtrusive. That's the whole formula.

**Q10:** What am I not asking that I should be?

**A10:** You're not asking about the emotional side. You're treating this like a usability problem, but for immigrants it's an identity thing too. Every time I have to convert inches to centimeters, I'm reminded that this country wasn't designed for me. It's small, but it accumulates. My daughter is growing up bilingual — Spanish at home, English at school — and now she's becoming "bi-measurement" too, which is fine, but I don't want her to lose metric intuition. When she asks me how tall something is, I want to answer in centimeters without hesitating. Also, you should ask about weight and temperature. Everyone focuses on length, but I also struggle with Fahrenheit — "it's 78 degrees" means nothing to me viscerally. And pounds for body weight, ounces for food... it's the whole system, not just inches. A good extension would handle all of it, not just length.
