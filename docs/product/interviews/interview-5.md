# Interview: Yuki S., 32
*Product Researcher, Osaka*

**Q1:** Walk me through the last time you had trouble with measurements on Amazon. Step by step?

**A1:** Oh, this was just last week actually. I was looking for a new portable SSD — the Samsung T9, you know? I found it on Amazon JP first, dimensions listed as "77 x 57 x 13 mm." Okay, clear. Then I checked Amazon US because the price was like 15% cheaper even after shipping. US listing says "3.03 x 2.24 x 0.51 inches." I stared at it thinking, "Is this the same product or a different size variant?" I had to pull out my iPhone calculator and convert each number one by one — 3.03 times 25.4... is that 77? Close enough? It took me maybe five minutes just for one product. Now imagine I'm comparing five SSDs across two marketplaces. That's when I start losing my mind. The worst part is I second-guess myself every time — did I multiply or divide? Inches to mm or mm to inches? I've made mistakes both ways.

**Q2:** What's the most frustrating part of how you handle unit conversion today?

**A2:** The interruption. Every single time I have to stop what I'm doing, switch to calculator, type in a number, multiply by 25.4, then go back and compare. It completely breaks my flow. When I'm in shopping mode, I want to be comparing prices, reading reviews, checking specs — not doing arithmetic. And it's not just one conversion. A typical product has length, width, height, sometimes weight in pounds versus kilograms. So that's maybe four to six conversions per product, ten products... that's fifty or sixty calculator operations in one shopping session. By the end I have a headache and I'm not even sure my spreadsheet is right. My partner Kenji looks at me like I'm crazy with all these numbers written on sticky notes next to my laptop.

**Q3:** What have you tried before? Why did it stop working?

**A3:** I tried FlyUnits — it's a Chrome extension that does unit conversion. But you have to select text, right-click, then pick the conversion from a submenu. For one number, fine. For fifty numbers in a shopping session? No way. I also bookmarked a conversion website, but switching tabs back and forth is even slower than the calculator. I tried making a Google Sheet with conversion formulas where I paste in numbers, but honestly copying numbers from Amazon into a spreadsheet is its own kind of tedious. My friend in a LINE group recommended a different extension — I think it was called Unit Converter — but it didn't work on Amazon JP at all, only on English-language sites. That's a dealbreaker for me because Amazon JP is my starting point. Nothing I've tried handles the real workflow, which is: I'm on a product page, I want to see both units right there, instantly, no clicks.

**Q4:** If a new Chrome extension claims to auto-convert measurements on Amazon — what builds trust? What makes you close the tab?

**A4:** First thing I check is Chrome Web Store reviews — specifically reviews in Japanese. If there are zero Japanese reviews, I worry it wasn't built with Japanese users in mind and Amazon JP won't work properly. Screenshots are huge for me. I want to see it actually working on an Amazon product page, not some generic demo. If the screenshots only show Amazon US, I'll wonder if it supports Amazon JP. The permission requests matter too — if an extension wants access to "all websites" and my browsing history, I'm closing that tab immediately. I only want it on Amazon domains. A clean, simple description builds trust. If they promise too much — "converts everything everywhere!" — I don't believe it. Also, if the extension hasn't been updated in over a year, I assume it's abandoned and won't work with Amazon's latest page layout. Amazon changes their HTML all the time.

**Q5:** What would you pay for this? What are you comparing it to?

**A5:** Honestly, I'd expect a basic version to be free — most Chrome extensions are. But if it truly works seamlessly on Amazon JP, US, and UK, and it saves me that thirty-plus minutes every shopping session... maybe 300 to 500 yen per month? I'm comparing it to my currency conversion extension, which was free, and to Kakaku.com price alerts, which are also free. The bar for paid browser extensions is very high in Japan. Most people I know won't pay for extensions at all. A one-time payment of maybe 1,000 to 1,500 yen would feel more natural to me than a subscription. I already pay for Amazon Prime in two countries — I don't want another monthly charge. If it had a free tier that covers basic conversion and a paid tier with something extra like side-by-side comparison view, that might work.

**Q6:** Who else in your life deals with this? How differently?

**A6:** My partner Kenji doesn't shop cross-border at all — he thinks it's too complicated, and honestly the unit conversion problem is a big reason why. He just buys everything on Amazon JP even if it's more expensive. My friend Mika in our LINE shopping group is even more obsessed than me — she shops Amazon US, UK, and even Amazon Germany. She actually built her own conversion spreadsheet with macros. But she's an engineer, so that's her personality. Most people in our LINE group of maybe forty people just avoid the problem entirely. They ask someone else in the group to check a US listing for them, like "Hey, is this monitor stand really 40cm tall?" It's become a community service at that point. My mother just doesn't understand inches at all — she once asked me "Is 5 inches big or small?" about a phone screen. Generational thing maybe.

**Q7:** Show me your phone — any related apps or tools?

**A7:** Let me show you. Okay so here's my iPhone home screen — I have the Amazon JP app and Amazon US app side by side in a folder called "Shopping." Then there's the standard Calculator app, which I use embarrassingly often for conversions. I have a unit converter app called "Tansui" — it's a Japanese app — but I almost never open it because switching between Amazon and a separate app is too slow. LINE is here, that's where my shopping groups are. I have the Kakaku.com app for price comparison within Japan. And Safari bookmarks to a conversion table I screenshot sometimes. Oh, and I have a Notes file called "よく使う変換" — "frequently used conversions" — where I wrote down that 1 inch equals 25.4mm and common sizes like 13.3 inches equals 338mm for laptops. It's my little cheat sheet. Honestly it's kind of pathetic that this is my system in 2026.

**Q8:** What feature would you love that nobody's built?

**A8:** I want inline dual-unit display. Like, right on the Amazon product page, next to "10.2 x 4.3 x 7.1 inches" it would show "(259 x 109 x 180 mm)" in a slightly different color, without me clicking anything. Just always there. Even better — and this is my dream feature — a side-by-side comparison mode where I can pin two products from different Amazon marketplaces and see their dimensions normalized to the same unit. Like a little floating panel that says "Product A: 259 x 109mm, Product B: 260 x 110mm — basically identical." That would save me so much anxiety. Nobody's built this because most extension developers seem to think conversion is a one-number-at-a-time problem. It's not. It's a comparison workflow problem. I want to compare, not just convert.

**Q9:** What would make you recommend this to a friend?

**A9:** If it worked on Amazon JP without any issues — like detecting Japanese dimension formats correctly — I would immediately share it in my LINE shopping group. That's forty people right there. The key is it has to feel invisible. If I install it, go to Amazon, and the conversions are just there without me doing anything, I'd be so excited I'd screenshot it and post it to the group that same day. Speed matters too — if there's even a half-second delay where the page loads and then the conversions pop in, it feels janky. And it absolutely cannot break the Amazon page layout. I've had extensions that mess up the "Add to Cart" button or make product images load weird. If that happens even once, I uninstall and warn everyone. One more thing — if it supports Amazon UK with stones and pounds for weight, that would be a fun bonus that would make my recommendation more enthusiastic.

**Q10:** What am I not asking that I should be?

**A10:** You're not asking about the emotional cost. Shopping cross-border should be fun — I'm finding deals, discovering products not available in Japan, it's like treasure hunting. But the unit conversion friction makes it stressful instead of fun. There's real anxiety. I told you about the monitor arm disaster — that cost me about 8,000 yen in return shipping plus the emotional frustration of feeling stupid. Every time I buy something from Amazon US now, there's this little voice saying "Did you check the dimensions? Are you sure?" That anxiety is the real problem, not just the time. Also, you should ask about weight and volume conversions too — fluid ounces versus milliliters for kitchen products, pounds versus kilograms for luggage. It's not just length. And maybe ask about screen sizes — "27-inch monitor" means nothing intuitive to me. I wish I could see that as actual width in centimeters. The inch-based screen diagonal convention is honestly one of the most confusing things in global electronics.
