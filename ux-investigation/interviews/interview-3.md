# Interview: Priya N., 41
*Interior Designer, Melbourne*

**Q1:** Walk me through the last time you had trouble with measurements on Amazon. Step by step?

**A1:** Right, so last month I was sourcing a sideboard for a client's dining room in Toorak — gorgeous Edwardian terrace, but the alcove is only 142 cm wide. Found a beautiful piece on Amazon US, listed as 55.9 inches wide. I copied that into my Google Sheet, ran the conversion — 141.986 cm. Technically fits, but that's 0.14 cm of clearance on each side, which is basically nothing once you account for skirting boards. Then I noticed the product listing had *two* different width measurements — one in the "Product Information" table and another in the bullet points, and they didn't match. One said 55.9", the other said 56.2". I spent twenty minutes trying to figure out which was correct, checking the manufacturer's site, looking at the product PDF. In the end I contacted the seller directly and waited three days for a reply. Three days of my client asking "have you found the sideboard yet?" That's the reality — it's not just a conversion problem, it's a trust problem with the data itself.

**Q2:** What's the most frustrating part of how you handle unit conversion today?

**A2:** It's the sheer tedium of it, honestly. I've got a Google Sheet template I've built over two years — columns for L, W, H in inches, then the CONVERT formulas spit out centimetres. But I'm manually copy-pasting every single dimension from Amazon into that sheet. For a typical project I might evaluate thirty to fifty products, and each one has three to six dimensions depending on whether it's a table with leg height or a sofa with seat depth. The maths isn't hard, it's the manual data entry that kills me. And the worst bit? When Amazon formats dimensions inconsistently — sometimes it's "L x W x H," sometimes "W x D x H," sometimes they use quotation marks for inches, sometimes they spell out "inches." My formulas break if I paste the wrong format. I've lost entire afternoons to this. My hourly rate is $150, and I'm spending it on copy-paste work that a computer should handle.

**Q3:** What have you tried before? Why did it stop working?

**A3:** I tried Everything Metric — that Chrome extension that converts units on the page. It was a disaster for my use case. It converted *everything*: prices, model numbers, review counts. The page became unreadable. I'd see a product called the "Model 3048" and it would try to convert "3048" into metric. It also rounded aggressively — I'd see "140 cm" when the actual conversion was 141.7 cm. That 1.7 cm matters enormously when you're fitting furniture into an alcove. I also tried a unit converter app on my phone, but that's even slower than the spreadsheet because I'm switching between devices. For a while I used Siri — "Hey Siri, convert 55.9 inches to centimetres" — but doing that fifty times in a row while my husband is trying to watch telly didn't go down well. The spreadsheet is clunky but it's the only thing I trust because I control the precision.

**Q4:** If a new Chrome extension claims to auto-convert measurements on Amazon — what builds trust? What makes you close the tab?

**A4:** First thing — show me the decimal places. If it rounds to the nearest centimetre, I'm out. I need at least one decimal point, ideally two. Second, I want to see the original imperial measurement *alongside* the metric one, not replaced. I need to verify. If it replaces the original, how do I know the conversion is right? I'd also want to see it work on five or six products I already know the dimensions for — products I've already converted manually — so I can cross-check. What makes me close the tab? If the extension asks for permissions it shouldn't need, like access to my browsing history or other tabs. If the screenshots on the Chrome Web Store look like mockups rather than real Amazon pages. And honestly, if it's made by someone with no reviews and no website, I'm nervous. I've been burned by extensions that stop getting updated after six months.

**Q5:** What would you pay for this? What are you comparing it to?

**A5:** Look, I already pay for Figma, Adobe Creative Cloud, my AIDA membership, Pinterest business — the subscription fatigue is real. But if something genuinely saved me that 20-30 minutes per sourcing session, and I do maybe three sessions a week? That's potentially 90 minutes a week, which at my rate is over $200 worth of time. So I'd pay... maybe $5 to $8 a month? I'm comparing it to what I'd pay for a good Figma plugin, which is usually a one-time $10-$20 or a small monthly fee. If it were a one-time purchase of $25-$30 I'd probably just buy it outright and not think twice. The free tier would need to actually work, though — not be crippled to the point of uselessness just to force an upgrade. I hate that.

**Q6:** Who else in your life deals with this? How differently?

**A6:** My colleague Sarah runs a kitchen and bath design studio, also here in Melbourne, and she has the exact same problem but worse because she's dealing with plumbing fixtures where a millimetre matters. She actually hired a VA in the Philippines to do the conversions and build spec sheets for her. Pays them $8 an hour, few hours a week. I've thought about it but I don't trust someone else to get the precision right — that's my professional liability, not theirs. My husband's a civil engineer and he thinks I'm mad for not just using Python scripts, but I'm not a programmer. He wrote me one once and it broke the first time Amazon changed their page layout. There are also builders and contractors I work with who just eyeball the conversion — "an inch is about two and a half centimetres" — and I want to scream. That's how you end up with a $4,000 bathtub that doesn't fit through the bathroom door.

**Q7:** Show me your phone — any related apps or tools?

**A7:** Let me pull it up... right, so I've got the Amazon app obviously, but I never source on it because the product dimensions are even harder to find on mobile. I've got a unit converter app called "Convert Units" — the green icon one — but I barely use it anymore, it's too slow for bulk work. I've got Google Sheets on here which is where I do most of my conversion work, usually on my laptop though. Pinterest for inspiration boards, Houzz for browsing — though I use Houzz more for seeing what other designers are doing than for sourcing. Oh, and I've got a measuring app from Bosch that connects to my laser measure via Bluetooth, so I can capture room dimensions on-site and they sync to my phone in metric. The irony is that my room measurements are beautifully precise in millimetres, and then I'm trying to match them against Amazon dimensions that say "approximately 22 inches." Approximately!

**Q8:** What feature would you love that nobody's built?

**A8:** A "fit check." I want to enter my room dimensions — say, an alcove that's 142 cm wide, 45 cm deep, 90 cm tall — and then as I browse Amazon, every product gets a little green tick or red cross telling me whether it fits. Not just width, but all three dimensions. And account for clearance — I always want at least 2 cm on each side for breathing room. Nobody's built that, and it would genuinely change how I work. Right now I'm doing that comparison mentally or flipping back to my spreadsheet constantly. The other thing? Batch export. Let me flag ten products, and export all their metric dimensions into a CSV or directly into a table I can paste into a client proposal. That would save me another hour per project, easy.

**Q9:** What would make you recommend this to a friend?

**A9:** If it saved me from a mistake. Genuinely. If I could point to a moment where the extension showed me a conversion I would have gotten wrong — maybe a product where Amazon listed dimensions inconsistently and the extension flagged it, or where my manual conversion had a rounding error — that's the story I'd tell. Designers talk, especially in AIDA circles and our WhatsApp groups. One genuine save and I'd be telling everyone. But it also needs to just work quietly and reliably for weeks before I'd stake my reputation on recommending it. If it breaks on one product page, or if Amazon updates their layout and the extension stops working for a week, that erodes trust fast. I'd recommend it when I forget it's there — when it's just part of how Amazon looks to me now.

**Q10:** What am I not asking that I should be?

**A10:** You're not asking about the emotional weight. This isn't just an efficiency problem — it's an anxiety problem. Every time I send a client a product recommendation with dimensions, I feel a knot in my stomach. Did I copy the right number? Did I convert it correctly? Did Amazon list it wrong? I double-check and triple-check because one mistake doesn't just cost a return shipping fee — it costs a client relationship. The Houzz horror story I mentioned, about the console table? That designer lost three referrals over it. Three. In this industry, your reputation is everything, and it can be undone by 3 centimetres. You should also ask about how we present dimensions to clients — I put metric measurements in my proposals, and if those are wrong, it looks like *I* don't know what I'm doing. The tool isn't just converting numbers. It's protecting my professional credibility.
